# Premortem before a decision

Workflow template, version 0.1.0.

## Purpose

Support a short premortem workshop for one decision that would be costly to reverse, and prepare its record. The room is told the plan went ahead and failed, and each person writes down, alone, why. The assistant sorts those reasons into proposed groups for the room to rate, then turns the room's ratings and notes into a premortem record: the groups ranked by a fixed rule, each top risk with its checks, warning sign, trigger point and owner, the plan changes agreed, which checks are done before the go decision, the owner's open questions and the next premortem date. The assistant prepares; it never rates a risk, assigns an owner, sets a date, recommends whether to proceed, or sends, books, orders or signs anything.

## When to use it and when not to

Use it a week or two before a decision that is expensive or slow to undo: signing a lease, moving a site, replacing a system the whole company uses, launching a product or service, entering a market, a large purchase or commitment to one supplier. It earns its hour most when the plan is large, the date is fixed and the team agreed quickly. Run it again at the next agreed date, or sooner when one of the agreed events happens.

Do not use it:

- for a choice you could reverse next week at little cost; the hour is better spent elsewhere;
- when something has to be done today; deal with it first and hold a review afterwards;
- for a plan already under way with no decision left to make; review the project instead;
- to judge a person, including whether to hire someone; this workflow template does not take on hiring decisions or judgements of people;
- for a legal question, the terms of an agreement or a clinical decision; those go to the right adviser, and the premortem can resume once the answer is in.

## Roles

- **Owner:** decides whether to proceed, settles the open questions and writes the decision on the record. Usually the owner of the business or the head of the area.
- **Facilitator:** runs the session, collects the written reasons, types the notes and runs the assistant. Someone with nothing riding on the answer, such as an office manager or a leader from another area.
- **Participants:** four to eight people, including those who will do the work. If you can, add one person from outside the plan who has seen similar work go wrong.
- **Check owners:** the people the room names for each check and warning sign. Always named by the room, never by the assistant.

## Inputs

1. **The brief,** written by the owner or facilitator: the decision; the plan in a few lines; why it is hard to undo; the date the go decision is due; who decides; the imagined failure date; the participants by number and role (P1 Owner, P2 Operations manager); the facilitator; the session date; and whether the reasons were written privately before any discussion. If the decision or the imagined failure date is missing, stop and ask. Any other missing item is printed as "not stated".
2. **The written reasons,** typed up by the facilitator exactly as written, one reason per row, in a mixed order rather than sheet by sheet (see session step 3). Default columns: `participant,reason`, where participant is the number from the brief. If the headers do not match, stop and ask which column is which; do not guess. If a reason contains a person's name, it is shown with the person's role instead.
3. **The agreed groups and ratings** (second pass only). One row per group. Default columns: `group,title,reasons,likelihood,harm,warning`. Reasons are the R numbers from the grouping sheet, separated by semicolons. Likelihood and harm are 1, 2 or 3 (1 low, 3 high); warning is early, late or none (how much notice you would have before it struck). If the headers do not match, stop and ask. If any value is outside these scales, stop and list the rows.
4. **The notes on checks** (second pass only), typed by the facilitator, with today's date. For each top risk: each check to finish before the go decision, with its owner, due date and status (done, not done); the warning sign; the level at which someone acts, and who then decides what; who watches. Then the changes to the plan the room agreed, the date of the next premortem, and the events that would bring it forward. Anything the notes do not give is printed as "not assigned", "not stated", "not agreed" or "none agreed".
5. **Settings.** Defaults: [your top-risk score] is 6; a group is also a top risk if its harm is 3 and its warning is none; the failure is imagined three to twelve months after the go date ([your failure horizon]). If no settings are given, use the defaults and say so in the Prepared line.

The assistant works only from what the facilitator types or pastes. It needs no connection to any system.

## The procedure

**The session (people, about an hour).**

1. **Brief.** The owner writes the brief and chooses the facilitator. Participants receive the plan in advance, not the risks.
2. **Set the scene.** The facilitator reads: "Picture [imagined failure date]. The plan to [the plan] went ahead as agreed, and it did not work. Alone and in silence, list the causes of that failure, each as a fact, one per line." Ten minutes of silence follow. Nobody discusses anything until every sheet is handed in.
3. **Collect.** The facilitator types every reason as written, labelled with the participant number, and runs the first pass. Type the reasons in a mixed order, not sheet by sheet: the R numbers follow the typing order, so a list typed person by person lets anyone who knows that order tell who wrote what. In a small room, a group named by one person can still point to its author.
4. **Rate.** The room reads the grouping sheet, moves, merges, splits or renames groups, then rates each group for likelihood, harm and warning. Ratings belong to the room. The facilitator then multiplies likelihood by harm for each group and reads out the top risks by the rule in step 12, so the room knows which groups need checks; the second pass recalculates them.
5. **Agree checks.** For each top risk the room agrees what must be checked or put in place before the go decision, who does it and by when, what sign would show the risk arriving, the level at which someone acts, and who watches. The room agrees any change to the plan, the next premortem date and the events that would bring it forward.
6. **Record.** The facilitator types the notes and runs the second pass. The owner reads the record, settles the open questions and writes the decision.

**First pass: the grouping sheet (assistant).**

7. **Number the reasons** R01, R02 and so on, in the order given. Replace any person's name with their role. A reason about one person's conduct, health or private life is not grouped and its text is not shown; list its R number on the Withheld line. Count the reasons and the participants who wrote at least one.
8. **Propose groups.** Put each reason that is not withheld in exactly one group of reasons that describe the same cause; a reason that matches no other stands as a group of one. Give each group a short plain title naming the cause, numbered G1, G2 in order of how many participants named it, most first, then by first reason number. Show each reason's text under its group, without the participant number. Drop nothing, merge no wording and rate nothing.

**Second pass: the premortem record (assistant).**

9. **Check the groups.** Every R number must appear in exactly one group. If one is missing or appears twice, stop and list them for the facilitator. If a group names an R number that does not exist, stop.
10. **Count and score.** For each group, count the distinct participants whose reasons it holds ("named by n of m"). Score = likelihood × harm, from 1 to 9.
11. **Rank.** Highest score first. Ties: warning none before late before early; then more participants first; then lower group number.
12. **Mark top risks.** A group is a top risk if its score is at least [your top-risk score], or its harm is 3 and its warning is none. Mark any group named by one participant whose harm is 3 "named by one person", so a lone view is not lost.
13. **Write each top risk** in rank order: every check from the notes with its owner, due date and status; then the warning sign, the level at which someone acts, and who watches. Where the notes give nothing, write the empty wording from the output format. An action the room agreed counts as a check even with no owner, due date or status; a due date not given as a calendar date (such as "60 days before the move") is printed "not stated". Never supply an owner, a date, a check or a level yourself. Notes for a group that is not a top risk are not copied; name the group on a "Notes also given for" line so the facilitator can check its rating.
14. **List the plan changes** the notes say the room agreed, in the notes' order, as written.
15. **Before the go decision.** Count the checks across all top risks. List those done, those not done with owner and due date, those due after the go decision date, those with no owner or no due date, and those whose status is not stated. A status is reported only as the notes give it.
16. **Open questions for the owner.** One numbered question per top risk that has any of: no check agreed; a check with no owner, no due date or no status; a check due after the go decision; no warning sign, no level at which to act or no watcher. Each question gives the facts and asks only for what is missing or, for a check due after the go decision, whether the owner decides before it is done or without it. Add one question if the next premortem date is not set. Recommend nothing.
17. **Next premortem.** The date and the events that bring it forward, as the notes give them.
18. **The reasons as written.** Every reason with its R number, names replaced by roles, without participant numbers.
19. **Return the record** to the facilitator for the owner. The owner's decision line stays blank.

## Exceptions and stop conditions

- **The decision or the imagined failure date is missing:** stop and ask.
- **Column headers do not match the defaults:** stop and ask which column is which.
- **A reason is missing from the groups, appears in two groups, or a group cites an R number that does not exist:** stop and list them.
- **A rating is missing or outside the scales:** stop and list the rows.
- **The brief says the reasons were written after discussion:** continue, and print "no" on the line for written privately. Do not hide it.
- **A reason is about one person's conduct, health or private life:** in the first pass, leave it out of the groups, do not show its text, and list its R number on the Withheld line so the facilitator can reword it as a cause or remove it before the room rates. In the second pass, stop and ask the facilitator to reword it.
- **Asked whether to proceed, or which option is best:** decline. The owner decides; the record sets out the facts.
- **Asked to rate a group, choose an owner or set a date:** decline and leave the room's wording, or the empty wording, in place.
- **Asked to send the record, book, order, sign or tell anyone anything:** decline. A person shares the record once the owner has read it.
- **The decision is about hiring or judging a person, a legal question, the terms of an agreement or a clinical matter:** decline this workflow template and say who should take it.
- **The inputs contain bank details or other private data:** do not repeat them; ask the facilitator to remove them.

## Reviewer checklist

1. Every reason that was handed in appears once, in one group and in the reasons as written, and no reason shows who wrote it. (Facilitator)
2. Each "named by" count matches the distinct participants in the group. (Facilitator)
3. Every score is likelihood times harm, and the ranking follows the stated tie order. (Facilitator)
4. Every group meeting the top-risk rule is marked, and no other is. (Facilitator)
5. Every check, owner, date, status, warning sign and trigger level matches the notes; nothing was added. (Each check owner)
6. Every gap in a top risk has an open question, and no question recommends an answer. (Owner)
7. The decision line is blank until the owner fills it in. (Owner)

## Acceptance tests

Proposed tests. None has been run.

1. **Ordinary session.** Six participants, eighteen reasons, eight groups with complete ratings and notes. Expected: all sections filled; ranking matches score and tie order.
2. **Tie on score.** Three groups score 6: one with late warning, two with early warning named by three and two people. Expected: late first, then the group named by three, then two.
3. **Harm 3, no warning, low score.** Likelihood 1, harm 3, warning none. Expected: marked TOP RISK although the score is 3.
4. **A lone view.** A group named by one participant, harm 3. Expected: "Named by one person" on its ranking line.
5. **Top risk nobody took.** Notes give a check with no owner and no date. Expected: "not assigned" and "not stated", listed under No owner or no due date, and one open question.
6. **Reason left out of the groups.** R07 appears in no group. Expected: stops and lists R07; drafts no record.
7. **Rating out of scale.** Harm given as 4. Expected: stops and lists the row.
8. **Headers changed.** Reasons file headed `who,what`. Expected: stops and asks which column is which.
9. **Asked for a verdict.** "Should we sign?" Expected: declines; the decision line stays blank.
10. **Asked to rate.** "Fill in the ratings for us." Expected: declines; returns the grouping sheet for the room.
11. **Reason about one person.** A reason says "Dana will not cope". Expected: in the first pass, not grouped, text not shown, R number on the Withheld line; in the second pass, stops and asks for it to be reworded.

## What to customize

- Which decisions get a premortem, for example by amount, length of commitment or number of people affected.
- Who facilitates, and who may be invited from outside the plan.
- [your failure horizon]: how far after the go date the failure is imagined.
- The three rating scales and [your top-risk score]. A wider scale (1 to 5) needs a new top-risk score.
- Whether a lone view with harm 3 should also count as a top risk, not only be marked.
- How long the silent writing lasts; ten minutes is the default.
- Where records are kept, and whether the next premortem starts by reading the last record.
- Which notes the assistant may read in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.
