# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. The method draws on the MIT-licensed maintenance planning package in kishorkukreja/awesome-supply-chain. From it: sorting equipment by how much its failure matters, with a risk to safety placing an asset in the top group, and fitting the kind of maintenance to the group, from repair after a breakdown, through work at set intervals of time or running hours, to work started by a reading; counting what a breakdown costs as the time stopped plus the repair; leaning on the maker's figures where the breakdown record is thin; mean time between failures and mean time to repair as the figures to watch; spare parts sized from what is used while a replacement is on its way; and a schedule grouped by how often each task comes round. Fully Powered AI left out the package's code, its statistical models of failure and of the best interval, and its sensor programme, and added: the register, task, history and parts files with set column headings and a stop on any heading that does not match; a failure cost from the history's averages or the manager's estimate, with the sum shown and cost bands; a reading that counts only with a numeric limit; running hours turned into whole weeks; an interval brought forward only when two or more failures of the same part come closer together than the maker's interval, never pushed out, and never changed on safety-critical equipment; a dated calendar for the year, with overdue and unrecorded tasks placed on the first day; at least one spare for top-band equipment and a flag for a part needed before it can arrive; a baseline for each measure with targets left to the manager; a separate section for the competent person; one decision per flagged asset; and questions where a breakdown names a safety device or matches no task. No third-party text is reused.
