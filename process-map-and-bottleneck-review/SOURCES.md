# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words, drawing on two MIT-licensed public packages. The process-mapper package in alirezarezvani/claude-skills, with its notes on bottleneck anti-patterns, contributed: timing every step and naming who does it, with waiting counted apart from working; treating work sent back to an earlier step as part of the process; a view of which role does which steps, from which the hand-overs are counted; a flag on every figure saying whether it was measured; describing the process as it runs now, one repeating process at a time; directing improvement at the constraint alone; and two further points, that an approval held for set days is a matter of turnaround rather than a shortage of time, and that work sent back is cheapest to prevent where the gap first enters. The package the-goal in glebis/claude-skills, with its notes on the five focusing steps, contributed: agreeing first on the single rate the process exists to produce; counting a step as the limit only if speeding it up would lift that rate; not trusting complaints to locate the limit; drawing more from the constraint's present hours before paying to enlarge it; an explicit list of steps not to work on; and naming where the limit is likely to appear next. Fully Powered AI designed the arithmetic that finds the constraint from each role's weekly load, the rule for counting send-backs into that load, the check of the constraint's capacity against what is actually finished, the way the hours each first option could return are counted, the stop conditions, and the rule that nothing changes until the owner decides. No third-party text is reused.

- **Fully Powered AI:** Fully Powered AI's own method for naming a process constraint: each role's weekly load computed from the hours it gives the process, send-backs counted into that load, the constraint's capacity checked against what is finished, the hours each first option could return counted from the inputs, and every change left to the owner.
