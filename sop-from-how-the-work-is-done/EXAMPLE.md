# Write an SOP from how the work is done: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* Larkmere Home is an invented 30-person online homeware retailer. Its people, systems, policy and figures are invented too. The inputs come first, exactly as the operations lead supplied them. The output is the complete reply they produced when the template was tried on them: a note on where the sources disagree, the draft procedure, and a closing list of what is still needed. The typesetting has been changed and one line of section 4 corrected, as the trial record says; nothing else.

## The inputs

**Input 1: the request and local settings, from the operations lead**

```
Procedure: Handling a return of an item damaged in transit
Owner: operations lead
Who does the work: the returns coordinator. She is on leave from
1 December 2026 and the warehouse associate will cover.
Prepared by: operations lead
Date: 9 November 2026
Sources: walkthrough with the returns coordinator, 3 November 2026;
note from the customer service lead, 5 November 2026.
Limits already set: finance policy FP-4 says the returns coordinator may
refund up to $150. Above $150 the operations lead approves first.
Review: every 12 months.
System names: the help desk, the order system, the payment system, the
carrier's claims portal, the Damage log sheet.
Redaction: roles only. No customer or staff names.
```

**Input 2: walkthrough with the returns coordinator, 3 November 2026**

```
W1.  A damage claim starts when customer service tags a ticket "Damaged"
     in the help desk and it lands in my returns queue.
W2.  First I open the ticket and check there are photos. I need one of
     the item and one of the box with the shipping label showing.
W3.  If there are no photos I reply with the photo-request template and
     set the ticket to Waiting. Nothing else happens until they come in.
W4.  Then I look the order up in the order system and check the delivery
     date. The claim has to be within 14 days of delivery.
W5.  If it's past 14 days I don't decide that myself, it goes to the
     customer service lead.
W6.  I ask the customer whether they want a replacement or a refund.
     Most want a replacement.
W7.  For a replacement I check stock. If it's in stock I raise a
     zero-value replacement order and the warehouse ships it the next
     working day.
W8.  If it's out of stock I offer a refund instead, or they can wait if
     they want. I sort of play that by ear.
W9.  For a refund, I do it in the payment system myself if it's under
     $200. Over that it goes to the operations lead to approve first.
W10. We don't ask for the broken item back unless it's over $75, then I
     email a prepaid return label.
W11. Then the carrier claim. I file it on the carrier's claims portal
     with the photos and the order value. It has to be within 30 days
     of delivery or they reject it.
W12. I put the claim number on the ticket and add a row to the Damage
     log sheet: date, order number, item, carrier, replacement or
     refund, claim number.
W13. Then I close the ticket.
W14. Oh, and if it's the same customer's third damage claim in a year,
     I stop and tell the customer service lead before I do anything.
     And anything glass that's cut someone goes straight to the
     operations lead. I don't touch it.
```

**Input 3: note from the customer service lead, 5 November 2026**

```
N1. We tag a ticket Damaged only after the customer has sent photos. A
    ticket without photos should not reach the returns queue.
N2. Late claims, past 14 days: I approve them if the carrier's tracking
    shows a delivery delay. Otherwise I decline.
N3. The carrier changed its claim window in September. I think it's 21
    days now, not 30. Someone should check.
```
