# Write an SOP from how the work is done

Turn a walkthrough of how one piece of work is really done into a written procedure anyone on the team can follow: who does each step, how they know it is done, what to do when it goes wrong and when to stop and ask. The owner approves it before anyone relies on it.

*Capability template · Operations and process improvement · version 0.1.0 · [Capabilities Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/capabilities/sop-from-how-the-work-is-done/)*

## The outcome

The work that lives in one person’s head becomes a procedure the whole team can run. A colleague covering for the first time follows it step by step. A new hire can be useful in days. An AI assistant has precise instructions to prepare the work from. That is how one person’s way of working becomes the whole team’s, and how a team does far more than its headcount suggests.

The procedure describes the work as it is done today, in the words of the person who does it. Nothing is filled in to make it look complete: every gap, and every point where two accounts disagree, becomes a question for the owner. The owner settles those questions and approves the procedure.

## Who starts the work, and when

An operations lead starts it, or the person who does the work. Start when a procedure exists only as habit, before the person who knows it goes on leave or changes role, before the work is handed to a new colleague or an AI assistant, or when a written procedure no longer matches what people do.

One run covers one procedure. A larger process is split into procedures and written one at a time.

## What they supply

- A walkthrough of the work by the person who does it: a transcript, a recording’s notes or their own written account of one real case from start to finish.
- Anything already written down: an old procedure, a checklist, a form or the messages people send each other about it.
- The name of the procedure, the role that owns it, any limits already set, such as approval amounts or deadlines, and what customer or staff detail to leave out.

## What comes out

You get a draft procedure in ten fixed sections: purpose, scope, roles, what is needed before starting, numbered steps, exceptions, stop conditions, records, open questions and revision history. Every step names one role and the sign that shows it is done, or asks for it. Every decision shows where the work goes for each answer. Every line points back to the words it came from.

Exceptions and stop conditions are always present. Where the material describes none, the draft says so and asks, because a procedure that covers only the trouble-free day is the one that fails a new colleague.

## What has to change for your company

Name the owner of each procedure and who settles questions when they are away. Decide who may record a walkthrough and what customer or staff detail is removed first. Supply your own limits and deadlines; the template never guesses them. Agree where approved procedures live, how often each is reviewed and who approves a change. Use your own names for roles and systems. The approved procedure then serves as the job aid and as the instructions an AI assistant works from, on ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini.

## Start using it

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
4. Run it on past work first, using the reviewer checklist and acceptance tests in the template, before anyone relies on it.
